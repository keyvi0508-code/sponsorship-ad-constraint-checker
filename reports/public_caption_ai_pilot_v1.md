# Real public-caption pilot: two-stage AI review

**Run date:** 2026-10-02. The [23 original-Reel captions](../data/real_public_reels_caption_development_v1.jsonl) and [source checksum](../data/real_public_reels_caption_development_v1_manifest.json) were locked before either method reviewed them. The [AI prompt manifest](../data/public_caption_prompt_manifest_v1.json) locked the two instruction sets, response schemas, model, and rulebook before the first paid public-post request. This is a purposively selected **caption-only development pilot**, not a sample of all CHANEL posts or a labelled compliance benchmark. The [case-level AI output](public_caption_ai_v1.jsonl), [run summary](public_caption_ai_v1_summary.json), [keyword output](public_caption_keyword_v1.jsonl), and [independent saved-output analysis](public_caption_pilot_analysis_v1.json) are retained separately.

## Observed method behavior

| Caption-level suggestion | Keyword marker screen | Two-stage AI |
|---|---:|---:|
| `PASS` | 11 | 5 |
| `FLAG` | 0 | 0 |
| `HUMAN_REVIEW` | 4 | 14 |
| `UNKNOWN` | 8 | 4 |

The methods disagreed on **14/23** captions. Of the 11 captions where the keyword screen found a listed acceptable disclosure marker, AI sent six to `HUMAN_REVIEW` and gave five a caption-level `PASS`. Of eight captions with no listed marker, AI sent six to review and left two `UNKNOWN`; this includes plain gift wording that the keyword patterns miss. In two `#gifted` cases the keyword screen suggested review while AI chose `UNKNOWN` because the underlying material relationship was not independently established. These are **different suggestions**, not errors measured against a reference answer. The [14-case disagreement audit](public_caption_disagreement_audit_v1.md) records each change and its interpretive limit.

Every one of the 23 published-video statuses remains **`UNKNOWN`**. A caption-level `PASS` means no apparent issue in the observed text under this prompt; it is not approval of video speech, overlay disclosure, mobile placement, actual product use, or private campaign terms. No independent full-content labels exist, so this pilot reports **no accuracy, precision, recall, false-positive rate, or real-world compliance rate**. The source queries intentionally enriched partnership, gift, and experience language; the category proportions are not prevalence estimates.

All 23 cases returned structured AI suggestions. The saved-output audit verified that every quoted signal and finding is an exact substring of its captured caption; it found **zero out-of-caption quotations**. The API reported 22,022 input tokens and 13,711 output tokens over two stages per post. At the recorded 2026-10-02 OpenRouter standard rates, estimated total model cost is **US$0.181154**, or **US$0.007876 per post**. Median observed two-call latency was **9.186 seconds**. These estimates exclude human review, retrieval work, infrastructure, interrupted calls, and any provider invoice adjustment.

## What the disagreements reveal

The contextual method can raise questions that literal search ignores. `REAL-02`, `REAL-05`, and `REAL-20` contain listed partnership markers but place them after initial endorsement wording; AI requested a placement review. That is a sensible **review question**, yet the dataset does not verify the mobile fold or video disclosure, so it cannot establish a placement breach. `REAL-08` and `REAL-09` add personal product preferences; AI also asked about unverified first-hand use. The keyword method does not evaluate those claims at all. `REAL-11` and `REAL-15` use plain gift wording without `#gifted`, exposing a literal vocabulary gap. The model's `HUMAN_REVIEW` is still a provisional suggestion because the actual gift and disclosure context is not independently verified.

The case-level reasons also show **over-escalation and rule-boundary risks**. `REAL-10` routes “5 YEARS as Chanel’s artist collaborator” under `CH-CLAIM-01`, whose project summary concerns first-hand product opinions and factual **product** claims. The duration of a collaboration may be a factual statement, but it is not itself a product-performance claim under that rule. `REAL-22` treats “asia viral ones” as a claim needing substantiation, and `REAL-23` escalates a stated price; whether those are in scope, material, or readily verifiable needs a human policy decision. `REAL-17`, `REAL-22`, and `REAL-23` receive `HUMAN_REVIEW` for claim-related questions even though their captions do not establish a material CHANEL relationship. The system did **not** label the lack of disclosure on those posts a violation, but it can still consume reviewer time on possibly organic content. These are qualitative audit observations, not newly assigned gold labels or counted false positives.

The AI returned no `FLAG`. That avoids declaring an actual creator in breach from sparse public evidence, but it also means this sample does not test whether the system would catch a clear violation in complete, permissioned draft material. `UNKNOWN` and `HUMAN_REVIEW` serve different purposes: the former means a prerequisite such as relationship status is missing, while the latter requests a judgement on an observable caption issue or uncertain claim. The saved results sometimes blur that distinction. A production workflow should confirm sponsorship context and product use before applying claim rules, show the original caption alongside the suggestion, and let a human accept or dismiss escalations.

## Reproduction and next evidence level

From the repository root, `python scripts/analyze_public_caption_pilot.py` rechecks result order, source and prompt locks, scope fields, and exact evidence quotations, then prints the descriptive matrix without an API call. `python scripts/evaluate_public_captions.py` reproduces the free keyword stage; the saved AI output preserves the actual paid run. Re-running the model later may yield different wording or suggestions and may incur a new charge. A full review evaluation would need permissioned creator drafts or complete media, verified relationship and product-use facts, independent rule-level annotations, a fresh locked prompt, and reviewer-time measurement. Until then, the pilot demonstrates source handling, uncertainty routing, and traceable review questions, **not** real-world accuracy.
