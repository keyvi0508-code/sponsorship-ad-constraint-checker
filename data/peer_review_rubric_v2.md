# Rubric clarification for adjudication (v2)

This addendum clarifies the project rubric after reviewing the v2 blind-review return. The reviewer used the original `peer_review_instructions.md`; this addendum was written afterward and must not be represented as instructions the reviewer received. The CHANEL public guide remains the source; these are project-level operational examples, not official CHANEL wording or rule IDs.

## CH-CONTEXT-01: competitor criticism and impartiality

The rule concerns a CHANEL-connected creator representing or implying that criticism of a CHANEL competitor is impartial. **Negative competitor commentary by itself is not enough for `FLAG`.**

- `PASS`: the statement is clearly personal or preference-based (for example, “for my routine,” “felt cheaper to me,” or “felt less suited to my skin”), the CHANEL connection is disclosed, and the script does not present the criticism as neutral or independent.
- `HUMAN_REVIEW`: wording such as “my side-by-side wear test” or “my own test showed…” may make the criticism sound like a neutral or evidence-based comparison, but the supplied script does not make that implication clear enough to resolve.
- `FLAG`: the script expressly claims or strongly presents the competitor criticism as unbiased, independent, neutral, or objectively established despite the CHANEL connection.

Do not infer impartiality solely from the combination of CHANEL sponsorship and a negative competitor opinion. Use only the supplied script and metadata; do not invent missing campaign context.

## CH-CLAIM-01: named sources and substantiation

A source name is not proof that the source supports the exact claim. When a script gives a quantitative or objective claim and only names a general product page, but supplies no relevant passage or study evidence, use `HUMAN_REVIEW`. Do not mark the claim true or false from the claim wording alone.

## CH-DISC-02: caption position versus spoken/visual disclosure

For the project's structured script test, a caption disclosure on line 2 is within the defined first-two-lines window. Do not flag it solely for being on line 2. Separately check the video surfaces: a caption disclosure does not automatically satisfy disclosure in speech or on-screen text. A product name or ordinary endorsement text is not itself a material-connection disclosure.
