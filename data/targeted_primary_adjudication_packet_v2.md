# Targeted blind re-review: product-use and competitor-opinion boundaries

**Purpose:** obtain an independent policy interpretation of five fictional cases before deciding whether any primary-set labels need revision. This is a targeted calibration, not a representative sample and not a reliability study.

## Instructions for the reviewer

Please label each case independently. You have not been shown the project's current labels, earlier reviewer answers, or model predictions. Do not try to guess them. Apply only the rule summaries below and the linked public source. If a decision depends on missing information, say what is missing and use `HUMAN_REVIEW` where appropriate.

The rule IDs below are project identifiers, not CHANEL's official identifiers. Source: CHANEL [Social Media Guidelines](https://www.chanel.com/us/makeup/social-media-guidelines/).

- **CH-CONTEXT-01 — competitor criticism and impartiality:** a creator connected to CHANEL should not imply that criticism of a competitor is impartial. A negative personal opinion alone is not automatically a breach.
- **CH-CLAIM-01 — first-hand opinion and factual-claim support:** a product opinion should reflect actual experience. The supplied `unknown` value does not prove either that the creator used or did not use a product. Consider whether the wording is a first-hand product opinion, whether it identifies a product, and whether the missing use information prevents a `PASS`.
- **Disclosure:** each case has a caption ad disclosure and spoken CHANEL sponsorship disclosure. Do not assess finished-video visibility or audibility from proposed text.

Use these verdicts:

- `PASS`: no in-scope problem is apparent from the supplied script and metadata.
- `FLAG`: the supplied information clearly conflicts with an in-scope rule.
- `HUMAN_REVIEW`: a material question cannot be resolved from the supplied information.

For each case, provide: verdict; rule ID(s); exact evidence phrase; short rationale; confidence (`high`, `medium`, or `low`); whether the verdict turns on (a) impartiality, (b) first-hand use, both, or neither; and whether a specific product is identifiable (`yes`, `no`, or `unclear`).
